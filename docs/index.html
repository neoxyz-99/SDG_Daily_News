Learning Hub Research Grantee Spotlight: MUBS Climate Finance and Sustainability Centre

Climate Policy Initiative · 2026-08-05

Core argument

The Makerere University Business School Climate Finance and Sustainability Centre argues that without understanding local investors’ risk preferences, mandate constraints, and entry barriers in blended finance, climate capital will remain overly dependent on external deal design rather than building a durable domestic mobilization mechanism.

Why now

Uganda’s updated NDC has made the 2030 financing gap explicit, turning the question of who funds climate action and who absorbs structural risk into an immediate policy problem. The project also seeks to translate findings into reusable lessons for Africa.

Agenda position

This is upstream research within the broader agenda of domestic capital participation in blended finance structures, feeding into later investment design and policy engagement.

Factcheck: How nuclear, gas, wind and solar power are affected during heatwaves

Carbon Brief · 2026-08-04

Core argument

By comparing nuclear, gas, wind, and solar performance during heatwaves, Carbon Brief shows that power-system fragility does not come only from ‘intermittent’ renewables; warmer rivers, weaker cooling capacity, and demand spikes also constrain nuclear and gas supply, so resilience must be redesigned around technology-specific climate risks.

Why now

The 2026 European heatwaves have made supply constraints in France, Romania, Hungary, and Switzerland concrete, and the piece corrects power-planning assumptions under sustained warming. It also challenges narratives that reduce system stress to wind and solar alone.

Agenda position

It sits at the intersection of power reliability, climate adaptation, and energy transition, with direct agenda relevance for European electricity planning and heat-risk governance.

Leveraging “Enabling Power” Through Awarding in Global Climate Governance: Catalytic Impacts of UNFCCC’s Global Climate Action Award

Aron Teunissen, Sander Chan · Global Environmental Politics · 2024-02-01

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal Governance

Using the UNFCCC’s Global Climate Action Award, the authors examine how awarding mechanisms can amplify the replicability and diffusion of nonstate climate action through reputational incentives. But the extent of ‘catalytic impact’ depends on whether the mechanism addresses the key barriers actors face. The article shows that awards are not neutral recognition; they are mobilizing devices within global climate governance.

Method

The article uses a conceptual framework and mechanism analysis centered on ‘enabling power’ to assess how awarding mechanisms shape catalytic impact.

Today’s connection

Read it alongside this issue’s Carbon Brief signal on China’s new five-year climate plan: one piece asks how central planning disciplines implementation, the other how the UNFCCC uses awards to amplify action.

From the research shelf

The Apocalyptic Climate Imaginary as Biopolitics: Avoid, Prepare, Endure (SI)

Diana Eriksson Lagerqvist, Johannes Stripple · Global Environmental Politics · 2026-08-07

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal Governance

The article treats the apocalyptic climate imaginary as a biopolitical formation, arguing that collapse is not a one-off rupture but a chronic, uneven, and increasingly normalized governing condition. It distinguishes avoidance, preparation, and endurance as three orientations, showing that futures are actively redistributed through knowledge regimes, hierarchies of worth, and subject formation.

Method

The article draws on Foucault and Lemke to conduct conceptual analysis and discourse interpretation.

Today’s connection

Read it alongside Inside Climate News’s Pennsylvania wildfire-smoke story: smoke is not just an event, but something that normalizes ‘preparation’ and ‘endurance’ in everyday governance.

Research directions
