Interactive: Tracking negotiating texts at the COP17 biodiversity summit

Carbon Brief · 2026-10-12

Core argument

Carbon Brief’s tracker maps the COP17 and protocol texts and shows the Kunming-Montreal framework entering its midpoint check while 22 of 23 targets are off track; the real contest is over legal wording, committee jurisdiction, the division of labor across SBSTTA/SBI/SB8J, and parallel progress on the Cartagena and Nagoya Protocols, meaning the bottleneck is enforceable text and institutional coordination rather than slogans.

Why now

The Yerevan talks are about to begin, and this tracker turns a dispersed agenda into the text battles that will matter over the next two weeks. The midpoint review means parties must convert targets into negotiable, accountable draft decisions.

E3G argues that if AZEC 2.0 remains centered on fuel supply security, support for the Philippine power sector may lock into fossil-fuel fixes and short-term patching; a more useful package would align Japanese technology, the Philippines’ drive to reduce imported fuel dependence, and actual power-sector decarbonization through grid upgrades, storage, renewables expansion and financing design.

Who Shapes Air Pollution Governance? How Economic Development, Democracy, and Data Condition Stakeholder Influence in Six Majority World Cities

Ella Henninger, E. Keith Smith, Thomas Bernauer · Global Environmental Politics · 2026-10-02

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal Governance

Using six highly polluted majority-world cities, 63 semi-structured interviews, and 72 experts and stakeholders, the article compares how economic development, democracy, and data availability shape the influence of citizens, local advocates, economic elites, and country-external agencies over governments’ air-pollution efforts. It finds that governments remain primarily responsive to economic elites across development levels; external actors matter more in the poorest case, Dhaka; democracy strengthens citizens and advocates through electoral accountability and civil society mobilization; and data availability both opens influence opportunities and triggers government counterstrategies.

Method

The authors combine semi-structured interviews across six Majority World cities with comparative analysis of stakeholder influence under differing institutional conditions.

Today’s connection

From the research shelf

How Information About Disaster Vulnerability Shapes Climate Policy Preferences

Talbot M. Andrews, Richard Clark, Rebecca Perlman · Global Environmental Politics · 2026-10-02

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal Governance

Using original survey experiments in the United States, the article examines how information about disaster vulnerability shifts public priorities between adaptation and mitigation. It finds that weak-prior respondents told about their exposure to extreme weather prioritize local adaptation over mitigation, while strong-prior respondents remain largely unmoved. The effects are especially consistent among independents, suggesting that mobilizing support for mitigation is harder when adaptation and mitigation compete for scarce budgets.

Method

The authors run original survey experiments in the United States to estimate how vulnerability information causally affects policy preferences across prior attitudes.

Today’s connection

Read alongside this issue’s Corpus Christi desalination story, as both show how adaptation projects run into fiscal constraints, risk perceptions and political support limits.
