Interactive: Who wants what at the COP31 climate change summit

Carbon Brief2026-11-02

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal GovernanceSustainable Finance

Core argument

Carbon Brief compiles around 100 documents to map how parties at COP31, under the Australia-Turkey joint presidency, line up on climate finance, just transition, mitigation, adaptation and the global stocktake, showing that the real contest has shifted from broad ambition to mechanism boundaries, financing conditions and issue linkage.

Why now

The Antalya conference is about to open, while war, an energy crisis and climate extremes are raising pressure for finance and implementation. It directly tests whether the post-COP30 promise of an “implementation COP” can be made real.

Agenda position

This is the agenda map for COP31 formal negotiations and the baseline text for positioning around the just transition mechanism, climate finance and the action agenda.

COP17: Key outcomes agreed at the UN biodiversity conference in Yerevan

Carbon Brief2026-10-31

Climate & EnvironmentBiodiversity

Core argument

Carbon Brief argues that COP17 completed the first global review of progress toward the 2030 nature targets, but the “deep concern” outcome shows most of the 23 targets are still off track; funding gaps, disputes over who should contribute, and a nearly empty fund expose structural weaknesses in biodiversity governance around finance, monitoring and responsibility allocation.

Why now

This is the midpoint review of the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, with just over four years left to 2030. Countries have signed the key texts, but the implementation gap has been put squarely on the table.

Agenda position

This is the post-COP17 outcome summary feeding directly into COP18 preparation and the midterm accountability process for the Global Biodiversity Framework.

Challenges of the OECD’s Tax Governance Regime Leading to the United Nations’ ‘(Re)entry’ into International Tax Law Governance

Jonathan R. Everhart · Global Governance: A Review of Multilateralism and International Organizations2026-09-30

Global Governance

The article argues that although the OECD-led GloBE rules created an inclusive framework, the governance and rule-making process still favors member states, making the arrangement insufficiently beneficial for developing countries; it therefore proposes a stronger UN role in international tax governance and a UN High-level Political Forum on International Taxation.

Method

The article uses institutional analysis to compare the inclusiveness and distributive effects of OECD governance arrangements with those under the UN; the available abstract does not specify a quantitative method.

Today’s connection

Read alongside Carbon Brief’s COP31 coverage to see how multilateral rule-making faces similar disputes over procedural inclusion in climate finance and just transition.

National Institutions and Global Public Goods: Are Democracies More Cooperative in Climate Change Policy?

Michèle B. Bättig, Thomas Bernauer · International Organization2009-04-15

Global Governance

The article tests whether democracies provide more global public goods and finds that democratic states make stronger climate policy commitments, but emissions outcomes are not consistently better; domestic institutions can raise policy output without necessarily overcoming free-riding, discounting and sectoral constraints such as transport.

Method

The authors use cross-sectional data for 185 countries from 1990 to 2004 to compare democracy levels with climate policy commitments and emissions outcomes.

Today’s connection

Read alongside this week’s E3G item on the UK budget and electricity-market reform, which also grapples with the gap between policy commitments and real-world emissions or price outcomes.

Research directions

When do domestic institutions translate into emissions cuts? democracy climate policy policy output emissions outcomes Why do commitments and outcomes diverge? global public goods free-riding climate governance transport sector
