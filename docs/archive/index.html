Governance legitimacy is expanding, but is responsibility being pushed onto the weaker link?

The Makerere University Business School Climate Finance and Sustainability Centre argues that without understanding local investors’ risk preferences, mandate constraints, and entry barriers in blended finance, climate capital will remain overly dependent on external deal design rather than building a durable domestic mobilization mechanism.

Issue · August 3, 2026

Climate risk is being managed through both technology and institutions: whoever defines what is “feasible” also shapes the boundaries of transition.

Climate Policy Initiative’s Learning Hub, by commissioning Genesis Analytics to study pension and insurance capital in Kenya, Nigeria, and Namibia, argues that Africa’s climate-finance bottleneck is not only a capital gap but a regulatory one: accounting rules and capital requirements keep domestic institutional investors anchored in sovereign debt rather than renewable energy, green hydrogen, water security, and resilient transport; the piece therefore points toward a blended-finance design that can work through regulatory constraints.

Issue · July 28, 2026

Climate governance is shifting from mitigation pledges to a redefinition of security, finance, and burden-sharing; who pays remains unresolved.

By co-chairing the new Climate Security Taskforce between the climate and defense ministries, the UK is linking extreme weather, supply-chain fragility, and defense planning into one governance chain; the piece implies that leaving climate risk within a single environment ministry will not produce the budgetary, diplomatic, or early-warning tools needed to act.

Issue · July 27, 2026

Heatwaves and negotiations are intensifying at once: are shocks widening the policy window, or exposing a governance mismatch?

Europe’s heatwaves are not being ‘caused’ by cleaner air; greenhouse-gas-driven warming remains the main driver, while falling pollution only removes part of the masking effect. The policy implication is that climate mitigation, air-quality policy, and public communication must be separated rather than blamed on net-zero transition.

05

Issue · July 20, 2026

When governance fails and finance is counted differently, who gets to define the costs of “adaptation” and “transition”?

The Commission’s ETS proposal weakens the expected stability of carbon pricing and complementary policy for industrial decarbonisation, raising capital costs and lengthening payback periods for firms already investing in transition. The implied argument is that co-legislators should restore investment predictability through a clearer, stronger policy mix, and anchor industrial competitiveness in stable decarbonisation rules rather than deregulation.
