Sustainable FinanceBiodiversity

Making nature investable through agrifood systems

Climate Policy Initiative · 2026-08-14

Core argument

CPI argues that the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework sets macro-level goals for nature finance, but capital still cannot flow into agrifood systems because investors lack comparable, actionable signals for highly localized ecological outcomes; the result is a need for fit-for-purpose nature measurement tailored to geography, sector, and investment type to connect ecosystem integrity, supply-chain resilience, and the investment case.

Why now

The piece responds to the run-up to COP30, the spread of TNFD-aligned reporting, and the consolidation of MDB principles for nature finance. It pushes the agenda from disclosure toward investable outcomes, where the next contest is who defines measurable nature performance.

Agenda position

This sits at the technical-institutional interface where nature finance moves from frameworks and disclosure toward allocation standards, within the pre-COP30 mobilization agenda for natural capital.

Sustainable Finance

Learning Hub Research Grantee Spotlight: I&P Ecosystems

Climate Policy Initiative · 2026-08-13

Core argument

Using I&P Ecosystems’ research, CPI argues that small gender-climate funds in Sub-Saharan Africa struggle to scale not for lack of ambition, but because blended finance tools, concessional capital structures, and coordination mechanisms remain unstable, preventing catalytic capital from reliably reaching women entrepreneurs, smallholder farmers, and climate-affected communities.

Why now

The study is embedded in the urgent debate over Africa’s 2030 climate-resilience financing gap and the recent policy window in which gender and climate finance are being emphasized together. It shifts the question from whether more money is needed to how concessional capital should be structured and coordinated.

Agenda position

This is a methodological and allocation-rule problem in the catalytic-capital ecosystem, positioned at the intersection of regional funds, gender mainstreaming, and climate-finance instrument design.

Climate & EnvironmentBiodiversity

Why land-use emissions have fallen by a third this century – in six charts

Carbon Brief · 2026-08-17

Core argument

Carbon Brief’s charts show that lower land-use emissions do not mean the governance task is finished; they indicate that policy and market pressure have begun to alter trajectories for deforestation, peatland loss, and forest degradation, raising the question of whether the decline is sufficient to support faster nature-climate co-governance or will be offset by rebound emissions elsewhere.

Why now

The piece uses century-scale data to revisit land-use emissions, responding to the question of how countries should interpret “progress” in the next round of NDCs, nature targets, and forest commitments. It also provides a time reference for whether the land sector can keep serving as a mitigation buffer before 2030.

Agenda position

This is an evidence interface between the NDC process and forest governance, sitting at the intersection of global nature targets and national mitigation commitments.

Orchestrating Global Climate Governance Through Data: The UNFCCC Secretariat and the Global Climate Action Platform

Laura Mai, Joshua Philipp Elsässer · Global Environmental Politics · 2022-11-10

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal Governance

Using the Global Climate Action Portal as a case, the authors show how the UNFCCC Secretariat moved from using data to pressure state negotiations before Paris to using data to track and animate implementation after Paris, thereby reconfiguring nonstate commitments, data infrastructures, and governance authority. The article’s central claim is that data is not a neutral record-keeping tool but an organizing device in climate governance.

Method

The article uses a single case study with process tracing around the Global Climate Action Portal, combined with conceptual analysis of changing data, infrastructure, and actor configurations.

Today’s connection

Read this alongside this issue’s “Q&A: What does China’s 15th five-year plan for coal mean for climate action?” to compare how national planning and data-driven governance each shape climate action.

From the research shelf

The Show Must Go On? Revisiting the Script of International Climate Cooperation Ten Years After Paris

Florian Steig, Eva Lövbrand, Angela Oels, Jonathan Barnes, Diana Eriksson Lagerqvist, Marie Fischer, et al. · Global Environmental Politics · 2026-08-01

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal Governance

Drawing on Foucault’s political thought and climate governmentality studies, this special issue examines how global climate governance seeks legitimacy ten years after Paris amid geopolitical rivalry, anti-climate populism, and accelerating crises. The introduction identifies four practices that stabilize the regime: promising future improvements, governmentalizing new frontiers, normalizing overshoot and climate disaster, and funneling climate expertise.

Method

The introduction uses conceptual synthesis and normative-political analysis to map the regime’s power/knowledge mechanisms.

Today’s connection

Read this alongside this issue’s “Offshore Wind Will Struggle Long After Trump’s Attacks” to see how anti-climate politics travels from domestic arenas into legitimacy pressures on governance.
