Analysis: Lula presidency saved at least 20,000km2 of Brazilian Amazon since 2022

Carbon Brief · 2026-09-28

Core argument

Carbon Brief’s analysis argues that Lula’s government, by re-enforcing the forest code, restoring environmental enforcement, and constraining illegal logging networks, has prevented at least 20,000 square kilometres of Amazon loss. It shows that forest governance in Brazil is not about abstract pledges but about the institutional struggle among federal enforcement, agribusiness interests, and electoral competition, implying the need to link regulation, budget authority, and constraints on local illegal economies.

Why now

The finding lands in the run-up to a new Brazilian election, when Lula and the Bolsonaro camp are closely matched and Amazon policy could swing sharply. It also pulls the slogan of “Brazil is back” back onto the terrain of domestic enforceable institutions.

Agenda position

This sits at the intersection of the Brazilian presidential race, forest-code enforcement, and COP climate commitments.

‘Concerning’ low sea ice persists as Antarctic hits third-lowest winter peak

Carbon Brief · 2026-09-25

Core argument

Using NSIDC data, Carbon Brief argues that Antarctic sea ice has stayed at unusually low levels for several years, raising the question not of one seasonal minimum but of a possible structural downward shift. That makes tighter links among polar observation, attribution research, and climate risk assessment necessary, because sea-ice change feeds back into ocean circulation, weather anomalies, and risk-warning frameworks in governance.

Why now

The piece follows the latest satellite release and stresses that the immediate task is not explaining one low reading but judging whether a new state has been reached. By juxtaposing Antarctic and Arctic ice, it also signals a shifting polar-risk narrative.

Agenda position

This sits at the intersection of polar observing systems, attribution research, and climate-risk governance.

Climate Governance Antagonisms: Policy Stability and Repoliticization

Matthew Paterson, Paul Tobin, Stacy D. VanDeveer · Global Environmental Politics · 2022-05-01

Climate & EnvironmentGlobal Governance

The authors set two common climate-governance strategies against each other: one prioritizes policy stability, institutional lock-in, and irreversibility; the other sees conflict and repoliticization as necessary to weaken incumbent power blocking ambitious mitigation. The article’s main contribution is not to endorse one path but to call for research on how stability and conflict interact in opening political space for rapid transformation.

Method

The article uses a forum-style conceptual argument and research agenda setting, drawing on a synthetic discussion of climate politics literature.

Today’s connection

Read with this issue’s Carbon Brief piece on Brazilian Amazon governance: does forest protection depend on institutional stability, or on electoral conflict that repoliticizes enforcement?

From the research shelf

Resilience, Adaptability and Transformability in Social-ecological Systems

Brian Walker, C. S. Holling, Stephen R. Carpenter and Ann Kinzig · Ecology and Society

BiodiversityFood & LandEnergy Transition

Walker and colleagues distinguish resilience, adaptability, and transformability to ask whether a social-ecological system should preserve existing functions, adjust its pathway, or deliberately transform when the old structure becomes untenable. The article links ecological feedbacks, institutional learning, cross-scale interaction, and actor capacity in one framework. Its key insight is that stability is not always desirable: some stable states lock in vulnerability and inequality. This helps interpret adaptation, biodiversity, and food-system policies by asking what should be maintained, what should change, and when transformation is necessary.

Method

The article uses social-ecological systems theory and conceptual modeling. It identifies system mechanisms, but concrete policy application requires additional case evidence.

Today’s connection

Read with this issue’s Carbon Brief report on persistently low Antarctic sea ice: is the polar system still in a stable regime, or has it crossed a threshold that demands transformation?
